The pitch

On a chain built by a brokerage, the memecoin is the guy working the fryer.

Robinhood Chain exists to put stocks and real-world assets on-chain — Wall Street's plumbing, pointed at retail. Every other token on it reaches for that seriousness. $KEVIN goes the other way and lands somewhere truer.

Kevin works minimum wage. He has WiFi. He clocked in at 3:41 PM on a Thursday in 2009 and has never clocked out. His plan has four boxes — LAUNCH, MOON, LAMBO, SLEEP — and he ticked three of them in advance, which he calls planning.

He isn't a joke about retail. He is retail: the person checking a chart on a break, with more conviction than capital. That person built this chain's user base.

And on the chain where a brokerage tokenises real-world assets, he opened a GME pool. He is not discussing it.

The one people will ask about

The holder round

There is a published list at /claim. It is real, it is checkable, and it is not open. Say all three or none of them.

What it isA GME round for $KEVIN holders, funded from the LP fees the pools have already earned — the same fees whose receipts are published at /#fees.
Who is on it17 addresses, published in full with their amounts and the Merkle root. Anybody can check any address without a wallet.
How they qualifiedHeld at least 5,000,000 KEVIN at the end of a fixed block window, weighted by how much and how long. Contracts excluded; a wallet that has merely delegated under EIP-7702 is not a contract and is on the list.
Is it open?No. The distributor is written and tested but not deployed. Nothing can be claimed and the page will not let anybody try.
Can I get on it?No, and neither can anybody else. The window it was taken over has already closed. The list is fixed and published.
Verify itThe round file names the exact command that rebuilds it from chain logs. Run it, and the root must match.

Never "hold to qualify". The snapshot is already taken, so it is also simply false — nobody can buy their way onto a list that is closed. It is the single easiest sentence to say by accident and the single easiest one to be held to.

If you write as him

The voice

He states absurd things in the flat tone of someone reading out a rota. He does not hype. He reports his situation, his situation is insane, and he has not noticed.

  1. First person, present tense, short sentences.
  2. Enormous ambition as scheduled admin. "Launching as soon as my shift is over."
  3. The job is normal to him. Because it is.
  4. Undersell. The bigger the claim, the flatter the delivery.
  5. Never explain the joke, never wink. He is not in on it.
  6. No "we", "fam", "guys", "ser", "anon". And no exclamation marks unless he is excited about something small.

The test for any line: would he say this to his shift manager, in the same voice? If it only works as a tweet, it isn't Kevin.

Five lines

What keeps the pitch bulletproof

Not a list of things to hide. A list of five claims that would be easy to make, are not true yet, and would hand a critic the whole argument. Avoiding them is what lets the rest land.

  1. No price talk. No targets, no market cap, no "next 100x". It reads as a pump to exactly the audience you want, and it is the one thing that turns a promoter into a liability.
  2. Never say floor. There is no defended price and no downside protection. Saying otherwise is a promise nobody can keep.
  3. No dates for the contracts. Built and tested, not deployed. "Built" is impressive and true; "live" is neither.
  4. The GME round is not open. The snapshot is taken and published at /claim, the distributor is not deployed, there is no date. Its window has already closed, so never "hold to qualify" — nobody can buy their way onto a list that is already fixed, and saying otherwise is telling somebody to buy.
  5. Nobody else's logo. Kevin works at McKevin's, and the sign says Mc — no arches, no golden M, and never a real chain's name, wordmark or mascot. Ten pieces of art were pulled off the meme wall for breaking this, and they are not on it because they were the funniest ones. Anything you make is yours; anything carrying somebody else's mark is theirs, and it is the one complaint that lands whatever else is true.

If you were paid to post, say so. Once, plainly, in the post. Undisclosed paid promotion is the thing that actually gets people in trouble, and disclosing costs nothing — audiences have never once punished it.
