§1Abstract

We present KEVIN, a fixed-supply ERC-20 token deployed on Robinhood Chain (chain id 4663), distributed by a four-day variable rate gradual Dutch auction, with sixty percent of supply committed to locked liquidity across three pools and zero percent allocated to the creator, the last of which is enforced by the token factory rather than by anybody's good intentions.

We further present Kevin, who works the fryer, has WiFi, and is confident that one of these is going to work out.

§4The three pools

Liquidity is split three ways, and each way is a different argument. All three are live and all three are indexed.

Three pools for one token means three prices for one token, which never quite agree. Bots close the gap and keep the difference. That is the job, it is not theft, and it is why a buy alert is sometimes followed one block later by a stranger doing something that looks alarming and is not about you. See §9.3.

§9Risk factors

No jokes in this section. Nine things that are true.

  1. It can go to zero. Most meme coins do. Assume this one will and size accordingly. Only spend what you would be happy to set on fire for the bit, because that is the transaction you are doing.
  2. Liquidity is thin. There is not much depth in these pools. A large order moves the price a long way, and moves it back a long way when it unwinds. That is arithmetic, not sabotage, and it applies to your buy exactly as much as it applies to somebody else's sell.
  3. Three pools means three prices. They drift apart and bots close the gap for profit. If you buy in one pool and immediately see a stranger's transaction in another, that is usually a bot doing arbitrage one block later. It is not aimed at you and it did not take your money.
  4. There is no floor. No price is defended. No contract owes you a bid. Nobody is standing underneath this. Anybody who tells you there is a floor is either confused or selling you something.
  5. Confidence is not a mechanism. Kevin is certain. His certainty does not appear anywhere in the code and cannot be redeemed.
  6. The GME airdrop is not confirmed. No date, no amount, no snapshot has been taken. It is an idea. If anybody tells you to buy or hold to qualify for it, they are not speaking for this project.
  7. Scammers will target you. Fake groups, fake contracts, fake support. He does not DM first, there is no allocation for you, and the only real contract address is the one pinned in the official group. Check it character by character.
  8. Nothing here is financial advice and none of it is an offer to sell a security. It is a joke on the internet with a website attached.
  9. Nobody is coming to fix it. There is no support desk, no recovery, and no reversing a transaction. See §12, which is shorter than you would like.

§11Errata

Things this project got wrong, kept in public, with the correction attached. Deleting a mistake is not the same as fixing one.

  1. The contract address was called the wrong token. A comment in the config once asserted the address was an old token on a different chain. Nothing had been checked before that was written, and it was then repeated back to the owner as fact — twice — telling him his own contract address was wrong. Checked properly afterwards from two machines, the address was correct the entire time. The comment is still in the config with the correction underneath it.
  2. Fifteen percent was written as eighteen. The liquidity list said fifteen and the sentence under it said eighteen, spelled out in words, so nobody searching for "18" would ever have found it. Both now come from one place, so they cannot disagree again.
  3. A contract could be tricked into raising the same number thirty times in one transaction. Found by attacking it on purpose, reproduced for about a hundredth of an ether, fixed, and the attack kept as a test that now has to keep failing forever.
  4. GTA 6 has been wrong four times. Kevin does not consider these errors. Kevin considers these Rockstar's errors.
